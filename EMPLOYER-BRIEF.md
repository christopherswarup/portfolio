# If you're considering me for a role

You probably don't need another polished bio. You need to know whether I can take ownership of a messy GTM problem, build the right operating model and improve the commercial outcome.

That's the lens I'd use to assess me.

I'm a senior Marketing Operations, Revenue Operations and GTM systems leader. I have spent 15+ years building teams, martech, lifecycle, reporting, planning and revenue infrastructure across B2B SaaS and enterprise technology.

I am currently available immediately for the right permanent, fractional or contract mandate.

## Where I tend to add the most value

### 1. Build or rebuild the operating layer behind growth

When Marketing, Sales and Product have grown faster than the way they work together, I can help rebuild the shared model underneath them — lifecycle, routing, handoffs, SLAs, ownership, systems, data and reporting.

I have done this in startup, scale-up, enterprise and post-merger environments.

Most recently at DevRev, that meant designing the GTM lifecycle and lead-management model across inbound, outbound, PLG and event motions, along with SLA governance, signal architecture and pipeline reporting standards.

Earlier in my career at Equinix, I was already doing the same kind of work before RevOps became a common organisational label — building global lead management, funnel reporting, database governance and a Sales + Marketing Ops forum to review volume, velocity, conversion and ownership across the funnel.

### 2. Turn MOPS / RevOps into a business multiplier

Operations should improve business performance, not become a service desk with better tooling.

I build teams around measurable outcomes: faster execution, better conversion, stronger pipeline, improved seller productivity, lower operating cost, better operating leverage and more trusted decision-making.

At Contentsquare, that included building a Global MOPS function supporting 120+ marketers across analytics, campaign operations, platform operations and lifecycle operations, leading 4 direct reports and managing a ~$1.8M MOPS and technology budget.

The point was not just to build a team. It was to build a function that could own the operating layer behind Marketing globally.

### 3. Simplify and architect martech around the business

I have seen what happens when every new problem becomes another tool.

At Contentsquare, I consolidated 100+ tools to 50 while reducing cost 18% and improving execution speed 30%. I also led major Tier-1 decisions including HubSpot→Marketo migration, Salesforce rebuild, Iterable rollout and the surrounding routing, data and enrichment architecture.

At Equinix, I owned a global demand-centre stack spanning Marketo, Salesforce, Terminus, BrightFunnel, Leadspace, LeanData, campaign automation, event technology and data-enrichment/orchestration tooling.

The principle is simple: fewer tools only matter if the remaining system works better.

### 4. Make planning commercially useful

Planning should not end with a budget spreadsheet.

At Nutanix, I owned the **$18M EMEA marketing planning process across 14 countries**, working with field, programmes, channel and digital teams to connect planned investment to expected pipeline and commercial outcomes.

I built the supporting operating rhythm: QBRs, monthly performance reporting, executive strategy decks, KPIs, forecasts and eStaff / Board-level dashboards.

That is how I think about planning generally: what are we investing, what do we expect back, what is actually happening, and what needs to change?

### 5. Make pipeline and reporting more credible

A dashboard is not useful if nobody trusts it.

I have built lifecycle, attribution, forecasting and reporting frameworks designed to give Marketing, Sales, Finance and leadership a shared commercial view.

At Contentsquare, I owned CMO-level funnel reporting and pipeline analytics; the unified Snowflake + Tableau layer improved forecast accuracy 30% and reporting reliability 40%.

At Equinix, I built lead-management and campaign dashboards across 13 countries. At Nutanix, I built the executive performance and revenue reporting used for EMEA QBRs, eStaff and Board discussions.

### 6. Use AI to improve real operating metrics

I am interested in AI because of what it can change in the operating model — not because every workflow needs an agent.

At Contentsquare, AI-assisted routing and prioritisation cut SDR response lag 40% and improved MQL-to-SDR velocity 35%.

My broader approach is to start with the business problem, fix the process and data foundation, then use AI where it can improve productivity, speed, quality or judgement. Adoption, governance and measurement come with it.

I also work hands-on with Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT, OpenAI Codex, Lovable, GitHub and Notion for research, workflow design, automation, technical problem-solving, prototyping and QA. I want to know what the technology can actually do before I put it into an operating strategy.

### 7. Connect Product signals to the revenue motion

This is especially relevant in PLG and hybrid GTM environments.

At Pleo, integrating product-usage signals into scoring contributed to +20% free-to-paid conversion. The point was not a more sophisticated score. It was getting Product, Marketing and Sales to respond to the same customer behaviour.

For a CPO or product-growth leader, this is the part of RevOps/MOPS I think often gets missed: product signals only create commercial value when the lifecycle, routing, ownership and follow-up model knows what to do with them.

### 8. Build campaign operations for leverage

Campaign Operations should make marketers more strategic, not create another production bottleneck.

At Nutanix and Equinix, I automated and standardised repeatable campaign work and built offshore production models for non-strategic execution. The Nutanix model doubled campaign throughput; the Equinix model doubled capacity and reduced turnaround time 30%.

The operating idea is simple: automate what should be automated, outsource repeatable production where it makes sense, and keep scarce internal capability focused on strategy, audience, proposition and performance.

### 9. Build governance that protects the commercial system

Data governance is not a hygiene project to me. It is part of revenue infrastructure.

At Equinix, I led the marketing-side database-governance workstream supporting GDPR and wider privacy requirements, alongside global data definitions, controls, data quality, lead-management and reporting governance.

At Nutanix, I later re-launched a MOPS data-governance workstream with Legal to re-audit 39 business applications for GDPR compliance.

The reason I care about this is commercial as much as legal: bad data breaks routing, segmentation, reporting, customer treatment and ultimately trust in the funnel.

### 10. Build teams, not dependency

I've built and led teams across multiple stages and geographies — from a 3-person MOPS team at Pleo to global COE models and distributed teams.

I care about strong operators, clear ownership and good systems. The aim is not to make myself indispensable. It is to build something that keeps working when I'm not in every meeting.

## What I care about commercially

I tend to come back to seven measures:

- **Impact** — pipeline, conversion and revenue contribution
- **Effectiveness** — doing the right work against the right signals and stages
- **Efficiency** — cost, duplication and unnecessary manual effort
- **Productivity** — how much useful work teams can do, and how quickly
- **Pipeline quality and velocity** — whether the system is creating and progressing the right opportunities
- **Confidence** — whether leaders trust the numbers and decisions
- **Operating leverage** — whether the business can improve output without cost and complexity scaling at the same rate

Tools, frameworks and process are means to those ends.

## How I work

I'm a builder first.

I like getting close enough to the detail to understand why something is broken, but senior enough to connect that problem back to growth, cost, risk and revenue.

I will challenge the software choice if the process is wrong. I will challenge the process if the ownership is unclear. I will challenge the metric if nobody can explain what decision it is meant to drive.

And once the design is clear, I like getting it built.

That can mean moving from a CMO/CRO discussion about pipeline and investment into the lifecycle rule, routing logic, data issue, system design or workflow underneath it. I don't think seniority should mean being too far away from the actual machine.
